Before you book

You cannot
get married today.

Not a policy of ours — Texas law. It’s the single most useful thing to know before you put a date on anything, and the thing we’d rather tell you now than at the door.

The Texas 72-hour waiting period

Texas requires a 72-hour waiting period between the time a marriage license is issued and the ceremony. You apply in person at the county clerk, both of you, with ID, and the license is valid for 90 days. There are limited statutory exceptions — ask the clerk’s office about your situation rather than relying on a website, including this one.

For a Dallas County license, start here: Dallas County Clerk — Marriage License ↗

Practical version: pick the date, get the license at least three days ahead, and book the chapel for after. If you’re coming in from out of state, give yourself a buffer.

What to bring on the day

  • Your marriage license, issued at least 72 hours earlier.
  • An officiant — or tell us and we’ll point you toward one who knows the room.
  • Two witnesses, if your ceremony calls for them.
  • The rings, if you’re using them. People forget the rings.
  • A group that understands the sign on the wall is a joke and the marriage is not.
